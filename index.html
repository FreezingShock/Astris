How a star is ranked

  • 1. All three. Links out, links in, and a similar name. Biggest, brightest, closest in.
  • 2. Two-way link. Each article links to the other.
  • 3. Link and name. One link plus a similar name.
  • 4. One-way link. A single link, out or in.
  • 5. Name only. Similar name, no link. Smallest, at the edge.

The Astris design system has two halves. Everything around the game looks and reads like an encyclopedia: a header with search, a side menu, tabs, bordered boxes, serif headings on a hairline rule, and blue links. The game itself is open space, where the same articles become stars.

Links

Link colour always means the same thing, on the site and on the map: an article that exists, one you have already visited, and one that does not exist yet. Links are never underlined until hovered.

Parts

liveplanned

Boxes have square corners, a one-pixel border and a tinted header. Nothing on the site casts a shadow or glows. Glow belongs to space.

Space

Astrıs Stars are light, not shapes. Colour is the subject. Brightness and size carry the rank. Threads are thin and additive, so where many cross, the map gets brighter.
